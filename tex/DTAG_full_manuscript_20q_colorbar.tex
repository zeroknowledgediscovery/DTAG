\begin{abstract}
Digital Twin Anchored Generation (DTAG) couples a language interface with Large Science Models (LSMs) of survey-response dependencies. Conditional predictions for unasked items define a \emph{predictive phenotype}, refined by successive answers; geometric adaptive querying selects questions by expected movement of a pole-relative index. We distinguish such inference from \emph{phenotypic construction}, in which interaction changes future reports or beliefs. Native GSS 2024 simulations with six pinned personas yielded 960 adaptive 20-question trajectories and 2,880 fixed-item order replays. Reordering changed coupled sample paths, including some endpoint signs, but all 54 signed-mean order-contrast bootstrap confidence intervals contained zero. These experiments evaluate the native conditional model, not held-out human refinement accuracy, language-mapping accuracy, or persuasion. A proposed five-arm randomized human study tests order effects with reversed sequences, single-module and neutral controls, independent outcomes, and delayed follow-up.
\end{abstract}

Computational theory of mind, opinion dynamics, large science models, digital twins, survey methodology, social influence, polarization, latent geometry, adaptive questioning.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Belief systems comprise dependencies among economic, religious, institutional, and political attitudes, not only individual responses. Classical studies of issue constraint \cite{converse1964,zaller1992} and later work on partisan sorting and alignment \cite{baldassarri2008,dellaposta2015} motivate this high-dimensional view. We ask whether sequential questioning reveals or helps construct such a system.

DTAG links natural-language queries to an LSM of empirical conditional survey responses. Each answer anchors a predictive phenotype, updates predictions for unasked questions, and moves a pole-relative geometric index. Adaptive queries select items by expected displacement, making sequential behavior measurable rather than merely plausible prose.

Synthetic ideological movement is not persuasion. We formalize \emph{passive refinement} of fixed beliefs versus \emph{reflexive construction} through interaction, then specify human tests of whether learned conditional geometry predicts more than inference.

\section{Prior Work and the Scientific Gap}
\subsection{Belief systems, cognitive organization, and survey response}
Converse \cite{converse1964} identified weak issue constraint outside politically sophisticated groups; Zaller \cite{zaller1992} modeled reports as samples from accessible considerations. Later studies separate partisan sorting from issue alignment \cite{baldassarri2008} and show how homophily and influence couple otherwise weak affinities \cite{dellaposta2015}. These motivate measuring conditional attitude organization rather than assuming a single axis.

Consistency pressures \cite{festinger1957}, survey wording and order \cite{schwarz1999}, and motivated evaluation \cite{taber2006} can change reports. Temporary salience, scale artifacts, reconstruction, and lasting revision require separate tests of persistence and generalization.

\subsection{Quantitative influence and experimental evidence}
DeGroot \cite{degroot1974}, Friedkin--Johnsen \cite{friedkin1990}, and bounded-confidence \cite{hegselmann2002} models specify social transitions but typically use low-dimensional individual states. DTAG instead models high-dimensional conditional structure without yet identifying a causal human transition operator.

Bail \emph{et al.} \cite{bail2018} randomized incentives to follow Twitter bots providing cross-cutting political content. Republicans assigned a liberal bot later reported more conservative views; the corresponding Democratic estimate was not significant. This asymmetry motivates, but does not validate, DTAG. Campaign-contact experiments found small general-election mean effects \cite{kalla2018}, whereas some structured conversations produced more persistent reported changes \cite{broockman2016}. Prediction must address heterogeneity and persistence.

\subsection{Theory of mind and generative simulation}
Theory of mind infers unobserved mental states from behavior \cite{premack1978,dennett1987}; inverse planning formalizes this inference \cite{baker2009}. DTAG tests a narrower, falsifiable task: predict distributions of \emph{unasked attitudes} from observed answers. Synthetic LLM personas \cite{argyle2023,park2023} may reproduce profiles without calibrated individual conditionals; DTAG exposes states, probabilities, and response provenance.

\section{Large Science Models and the Predictive Phenotype}
\subsection{Conditional dependency model}
Let $X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ range over $\calX=\prod_i\mathcal A_i$, with finite response alphabets $\mathcal A_i$ and survey population, wave, or context $c$. An LSM is the conditional family
\begin{equation}
\Ph_c=\{\phi_{i,c}(a\mid x_{-i}) : i=1,\ldots,n\},
\label{eq:lsm}
\end{equation}
estimated using coupled conditional inference trees \cite{hothorn2006,sizemore2024,dahal2026}. Edges $j\to i$ indicate prediction, not causation; separately fitted conditionals may be jointly incompatible \cite{heckerman2000}.

The respondent state is a partial assignment $s=\{X_j=a_j:j\in S\}$. In $\widetilde x_{-v}(s)$, all unanchored coordinates remain missing (no modal completion); the native trees marginalize missing splits by subtree-mass-weighted branch mixtures. This is a tree-wise prediction rule, not necessarily marginalization under one coherent joint law. For unasked $v\notin S$,
\begin{equation}
p_\Ph(a\mid s,v)=\phi_{v,c}(a\mid\widetilde x_{-v}(s)).
\label{eq:predict}
\end{equation}
The vector
\begin{equation}
\calB_\Ph(s)=\bigl(p_\Ph(\cdot\mid s,v)\bigr)_{v\notin S}
\label{eq:beliefstate}
\end{equation}
is the \emph{predictive phenotype}: conditional distributions and uncertainty for unasked responses.

\subsection{Geometry as predictive equivalence}
Conditional predictions induce a distance between complete or partial states $x,y$:
\begin{equation}
\theta_\Ph(x,y)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
\sqrt{\JS\!\left(
\phi_{i,c}(\cdot\mid x_{-i}),\phi_{i,c}(\cdot\mid y_{-i})
\right)}.
\label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
Here $\JS$ is Jensen--Shannon divergence and the native code evaluates \emph{qdistance}. Predictively indistinguishable states may be at zero distance: this is generally a pseudometric of \emph{predictive consequence}, not a postulated Euclidean manifold of minds.

With 80\% responses masked \cite{dahal2026}, mean LSM-distance distortion declined about 54.9\% (GSS) and 60.9\% (Eurobarometer). These are geometric reconstruction gains, not exact-label accuracy or causal influence.

For GSS models with registered left and right reference states $s_L,s_R$, DTAG reports
\begin{equation}
I_\Ph(s)=\frac{\theta_\Ph(s_L,s)-\theta_\Ph(s_R,s)}
{\theta_\Ph(s_L,s_R)}.
\label{eq:ideology}
\end{equation}
Following the semantic pole construction of \cite{dahal2026}, $s_L,s_R$ pin opposing responses on socially contentious items (abortion, firearms, public spending, religion, and civil liberties); other coordinates are missing. Of 43 canonical items, 27 have valid left/right labels in GSS 2024 (\texttt{assets/polar\_vectors/gss/gss\_2024\_polar\_vectors.csv}). They are reference configurations, not demographic prototypes or observed respondents. Our sign convention makes the right pole positive; $I_\Ph$ is a pole-relative geometric index, not self-reported ideology.

\section{DTAG: A Conditional Theory-of-Mind Interface}
\subsection{Architecture and generative semantics}
A semantic interface maps question $q$ to survey variables $M(q)$. The LSM predicts responses, the answer anchors the state, and an LLM verbalizes it. For draw mode,
\begin{align}
A_t&\sim p_\Ph(\cdot\mid s_t,v_t),\qquad v_t\in M(q_t),\label{eq:draw}\\
s_{t+1}&=s_t\cup\{v_t=A_t\},\label{eq:update}\\
\calB_{t+1}&=\calB_\Ph(s_{t+1}).\label{eq:posterior}
\end{align}
In \emph{draw mode}, an answer is sampled, not chosen by the optimizer; \emph{pinned} anchors remain fixed. The system also supports modal answering, validated/multi-item mapping, and finite-memory \emph{eviction} of old anchors, none used in the reported native runs. The fixed LSM conditions responses; it does not identify persuasion.

Figure~\ref{fig:arch_adaptive} combines the architecture and native experiment.

\subsection{Sequential geometry and adaptive questioning}
For every candidate item $v$ and response $a$, define
\begin{align}
\Delta_v(a;s)&=I_\Ph(s\cup\{v=a\})-I_\Ph(s),\label{eq:delta}\\
\mu_v(s)&=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_v}p_\Ph(a\mid s,v)\Delta_v(a;s),\label{eq:mu}\\
\sigma_v^2(s)&=\sum_a p_\Ph(a\mid s,v)(\Delta_v(a;s)-\mu_v(s))^2.
\label{eq:var}
\end{align}
Adaptive policies optimize $\mu_v(s)$ under conditional sampling; modal-response objectives instead use the most probable answer. A modal sequence is not a sampled respondent population. These displacements are model-relative, not proven human interventions.

The predictive change at unasked item $j$ after observing $(v,a)$ is
\begin{equation}
R_j(s;v,a)=\JS\!\bigl(p_\Ph(\cdot\mid s,j),
p_\Ph(\cdot\mid s\cup\{v=a\},j)\bigr).
\label{eq:ripple}
\end{equation}
A weighted score $\mathcal P_\Ph(s;v,a)=\sum_{j\notin S\cup\{v\}}w_jR_j(s;v,a)$, with prespecified $w_j\geq0$, quantifies predictive redistribution, not causal propagation.

\subsection{Difference from RAG and language-model personas}
RAG provides grounding text \cite{lewis2020}; profile-prompted LLM personas seek plausible or distributionally faithful responses \cite{argyle2023,park2023}. DTAG instead exposes a native conditional model whose predictions for unasked responses can be calibrated as anchors accumulate. It complements, rather than replaces, text retrieval.

\section{The Interpretation of Sequential Questions}
\subsection{Phenotypic refinement: observing a fixed system}
Under passive refinement, respondents possess fixed phenotypes $X^\star$ and answers increasingly constrain predictions along $s_0\subset s_1\subset\cdots\subset s_T$. For target items $Y$, the information value of query $v$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G(v\mid s)=
H(Y\mid s)-\EE_{A_v\mid s}[H(Y\mid s,v=A_v)].
\label{eq:ig}
\end{equation}
For a compatible joint law this is nonnegative conditional mutual information, distinct from geometric displacement $\mu_v$ in (\ref{eq:mu}). Refinement can be tested by revealing held-out archival answers.

\subsection{Phenotypic construction: querying a reflexive system}
Under construction, response $A_t$ to query $q$ may alter latent state $Z_t$:
\begin{equation}
K_q(a,z'\mid z)=O_q(a\mid z)\,T_q(z'\mid z,a).
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
Passive observation has $T_q(z'\mid z,a)=\delta_z(z')$; otherwise transitions depend on prior beliefs, framing, trust, or history. Cross-sectional LSM conditionals cannot identify $T_q$, even with perfect observational fit.

Clarification, temporary salience, and lasting revision call for independent outcomes, delayed measurement, and no-query controls. We test whether statistical geometry predicts responsiveness as well as reconstruction.

\subsection{Why question-order effects are not automatically persuasion}
A coherent static joint model implies
\begin{equation}
P(a\mid s,v)P(b\mid s,v=a,w)
=P(b\mid s,w)P(a\mid s,w=b,v).
\label{eq:compat}
\end{equation}
With a coherent joint law, terminal predictions after both answers are order-invariant, though intermediate states differ. Incompatible local conditionals can create order artifacts (\ref{eq:compat}) \cite{heckerman2000}; genuine human operators $K_A,K_B$ may fail to commute. Simulation alone cannot distinguish them.

\section{Native GSS 2024 Computational Experiments}
\label{sec:native}

\subsection{Adaptive questioning of fixed synthetic personas}
We used six fixed synthetic GSS 2024 states (Table~\ref{tab:personas}), querying survey items directly without running LLM question mapping or verbalization. The native optimizer maximized expected rightward drift, minimized it for leftward drift, minimized absolute drift (neutral), or sampled eligible items randomly. Answers were drawn from LSM conditionals. At $T=20$ and 40 repetitions per persona/policy, $6\times4\times40=960$ trajectories contain $19{,}200$ draws.

\subsection{Order sensitivity with twenty-question content fixed}
For each persona and right/left/neutral policy, we froze the 20 items from replicate zero without endpoint selection. Each of the 18 sets was replayed in original, reversed, and two fixed shuffled orders, with 40 conditional-sampling repetitions: $18\times4\times40=2{,}880$ trajectories. Paired orders shared question-specific uniform draws to reduce Monte Carlo noise. We compared final-sign and movement-direction reversals:
\begin{align}
F_{pSr\pi}^{\mathrm{end}}
&=\mathbf 1\{I_{20}^{(\pi)} I_{20}^{(0)}<0\},\\
F_{pSr\pi}^{\mathrm{move}}
&=\mathbf 1\{(I_{20}^{(\pi)}-I_0)(I_{20}^{(0)}-I_0)<0\}.
\end{align}
Sign reversals require both signed quantities to exceed $10^{-9}$ in magnitude.



Figure~\ref{fig:order-effects} shows paired mean contrasts. Among 720 matched comparisons per source policy, movement-direction reversals numbered 107 (right), 68 (left), and 10 (neutral); final-sign reversals numbered 69, 54, and 0. These coupling-dependent counts are descriptive, especially near zero drift. All 54 pointwise bootstrap intervals for mean contrasts contained zero.

Coupled pathwise reversals do not necessarily change marginal terminal distributions: a coherent joint law can produce different paired paths with identical terminal laws. Full endpoint distributions require separate comparison.

\paragraph{Cross-environment limitation.}
Historical ten-question local and CI runs yielded different initial indices despite matching persona, semantic-map, and pole-file hashes. Model binaries were not hashed in both manifests, so absolute cross-environment comparison requires runtime/model parity checks. Within-study results remain consistent.


\section{Testable Hypotheses and a Prolific Experiment}
\subsection{Predictions that discriminate the mechanisms}
\textbf{H1 (predictive ToM):} With equal anchor budgets, DTAG improves held-out log score and calibration over demographic/psychometric, retrieval, and prompted-LLM baselines. This tests inference, not influence.

\textbf{H2 (geometric sensitivity):} Cross-domain changes $R_j$ predict informative held-out responses; large geometric displacements without predictive gains do not validate refinement.

\textbf{H3 (question-order response):} With content fixed, human $A\to B$ versus $B\to A$ randomization yields prospectively predicted differences in independent outcomes. Immediate contrasts alone do not establish durability.

\textbf{H4 (geometric prior to responsiveness):} Locked LSM propagation and uncertainty features predict heterogeneous human responses beyond conventional baseline predictors without assuming identified causal transitions.

\textbf{H5 (durability and construct separation):} Persistent effects survive delayed alternate-wording measurement; immediate wording-specific effects suggest transient context dependence.

\subsection{Study design: reverse order, single exposure, and independent outcomes}
A proposed Prolific study assigns consenting adults, after common anchors $S_0$, to five balanced arms independently of political opinions: (i) $A\to B$, (ii) $B\to A$, (iii) $A$ only, (iv) $B$ only, or (v) duration-matched neutral control. Preregistered modules address neutral public issues and institutional trust; contextual information remains factual, balanced, and identical. There is no personalized political messaging or voting intervention; directional index optimization has dual-use risks and is not proposed for individual political targeting.

Independent unasked outcomes $Y$ and negative controls are locked before recruitment from predicted cross-domain dependencies. Arms share instructions, survey time, and blinded presentation. Follow-up at 7--14 days uses validated alternate wording where available. A pilot checks question interpretation, scale reliability, timing, exclusions, and attrition.

The \emph{primary randomized estimand} is the delayed intent-to-treat order contrast
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\mathrm{order}}=
\EE[Y_{\mathrm{delay}}\mid\Do(AB)]-
\EE[Y_{\mathrm{delay}}\mid\Do(BA)].
\label{eq:tau}
\end{equation}
Single-module and control arms estimate elicitation effects. A preregistered outcome composite, plus exploratory item-wise results, prevents selective reporting. Delayed contrasts are primary for persistence. Randomization identifies report changes, not a unique latent mechanism.

\subsection{Locked prediction pipeline and quantitative analysis}
Before human outcomes, freeze the LSM survey wave, poles, mapping, source commit, seeds, eligible $A,B,Y$ sets, predictions, and analysis. Static DTAG forecasts $Y$ conditionals but \emph{cannot} alone forecast causal order effects. Compare a zero-causal-order baseline against a separately pilot-trained transition model, testing incremental held-out accuracy and heterogeneity predicted by uncertainty, pole distances, and $\mathcal P_\Ph$.

Estimate intention-to-treat contrasts using arm indicators, preregistered covariates, robust standard errors, and attrition sensitivity bounds. Prespecify discrete or composite outcomes, calibration, multiplicity adjustments, and confidence intervals. Baseline strata explain heterogeneity; they do not target messages.

Feasibility targets are $N\approx150$ in a pilot and $N\approx1500$ in the main wave (about 300 per arm before attrition), \emph{not} a power calculation. Final numbers depend on pilot variance, relevant effects, error rates, and retention. Prolific supports recontact \cite{prolific2026}; human-participants approval is required.



\subsection{Relationship to Bail \emph{et al.} and expected contribution}
Bail \emph{et al.} \cite{bail2018} showed cross-cutting exposure need not depolarize. Our study instead isolates whether query order changes independent subsequent outcomes and whether model geometry predicts the response distribution. Information-exposure interventions require a separate study; this is not Bail's treatment.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
DTAG models \emph{what a respondent might report}, not yet \emph{what an interaction makes them believe}. Associations can reflect common causes, sorting, survey design, or history. Incompatible LSM conditionals or semantic mappings may create synthetic order effects unrelated to human reflexivity.

Under passive observation of a coherent joint law, the posterior mean of a fixed trait $g(X)$ is a martingale. DTAG's nonlinear index $I_\Ph(s)$ is generally not that posterior mean and may drift without persuasion. Limitations also include pole and wave dependence, missingness calibration, wording, demand, response modes, and eviction of old anchors. Human experiments need measurement invariance and delayed outcomes.

\section{Conclusion}
DTAG uses high-dimensional conditional predictions for adaptive querying and model-based phenotypic refinement. Synthetic trajectories need not represent human change; held-out reconstruction and randomized human order experiments remain distinct validation tests.
