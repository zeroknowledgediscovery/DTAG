\begin{abstract}
Digital Twin Anchored Generation (DTAG) selects survey questions using the conditional response structure learned by a Large Science Model (LSM) to direct movement in a pole-relative predictive phenotype. We evaluate this procedure in two response regimes. In the computational experiments, six specified GSS 2024 personas provide the initial states; DTAG chooses each subsequent question and draws its answer from the LSM conditional distribution, without generating persona responses using an LLM. Across 960 twenty-question simulated trajectories, directional policies produced the intended displacement without selecting any of the 27 pole-defining items; 2,880 fixed-set replays assessed the effect of question order. In the prospective human experiment, DTAG again uses LSM-based question selection, but actual participants answer the questions rather than sampling responses from the model. Natural-language answers are mapped to survey variables to update the predictive phenotype. Among 71 completed calibrated human sessions (21 Left, 38 Right, 7 Neutral, 5 Random), 20/21 Left-push participants (95.2\%) moved leftward and 21/38 Right-push participants (55.3\%) moved rightward. Rightward displacement occurred in 12/29 initially Left-leaning participants (41.4\%) and 9/9 initially Right-leaning participants (100\%). All 17 initially Left-leaning Right-push nonresponders were identified from their first four adaptive answers, with two additional participants flagged (accuracy 27/29, 93.1\%). These results show that adaptive inquiry can produce directional movement in an inferred survey-response phenotype and reveal direction-specific resistance early in the questioning sequence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Survey questions are usually treated as measurements, but each answer also changes predictions about unanswered items. We ask whether choosing \emph{which questions to pose} can steer a respondent's inferred ideological position. If so, adaptive surveys do more than collect information: their selection policies shape the modeled opinion profile.

Large Science Models (LSMs) provide a new framework for generative digital twins of multivariate systems. They learn interdependent conditional distributions, infer unobserved variables from partial evidence, and compare states through their predictive consequences. Related digital-twin applications include infant microbiome development \cite{sizemore2024} and influenza evolution through Emergenet \cite{wu2026emergenet}. DTAG applies an LSM to survey answers, defining a \emph{predictive phenotype} over unasked items and its position relative to opposing reference opinion profiles. It selects questions to maximize expected signed displacement while respondents supply the answers.

We test DTAG first in 960 native GSS 2024 simulations seeded by six stated personas, with answers sampled entirely from the LSM, followed by 2,880 fixed-set LSM replays. We then replace the simulated respondents with human participants: the adaptive questions remain LSM-directed, but the answers come from 71 Prolific participants and update the same conditional-survey phenotype through native-variable mappings. The human trajectories also reveal baseline-dependent Right-push resistance detectable from the first four responses.
\section{Related Work}
Attitude dependencies need not align on one ideological axis \cite{converse1964,zaller1992}. Partisan sorting and social influence shape these dependencies \cite{baldassarri2008,dellaposta2015}; cognitive consistency, wording, and order can affect reported attitudes \cite{festinger1957,schwarz1999,taber2006}. This motivates separating information revealed by a question from any change induced by asking it.

Opinion-dynamics models often use low-dimensional transitions \cite{degroot1974,friedkin1990,hegselmann2002}, while experiments on exposure and contact report heterogeneous effects \cite{bail2018,kalla2018,broockman2016}. Generative agents and prompted survey personas can produce plausible responses \cite{argyle2023,park2023}. DTAG instead combines learned high-dimensional survey conditionals with an explicit question-selection objective.

\section{Large Science Models and the Predictive Phenotype}
\subsection{Conditional dependency model}
Let $X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ be categorical survey responses, with alphabets $\mathcal A_i$ and survey context $c$. An LSM comprises conditionals
\begin{equation}
\Ph_c=\{\phi_{i,c}(a\mid x_{-i}) : i=1,\ldots,n\},
\label{eq:lsm}
\end{equation}
estimated by conditional inference trees \cite{hothorn2006,sizemore2024,dahal2026}. The learned conditionals supply both the question-selection objective and the updated predictive phenotype.

A respondent state $s=\{X_j=a_j:j\in S\}$ holds observed responses. Unanchored variables remain missing in $\widetilde x_{-v}(s)$; each native tree averages over its missing branches. For unasked $v\notin S$,
\begin{equation}
p_\Ph(a\mid s,v)=\phi_{v,c}(a\mid\widetilde x_{-v}(s)).
\label{eq:predict}
\end{equation}
The vector
\begin{equation}
\calB_\Ph(s)=\bigl(p_\Ph(\cdot\mid s,v)\bigr)_{v\notin S}
\label{eq:beliefstate}
\end{equation}
is the \emph{predictive phenotype}: conditional distributions and uncertainty for unasked responses.

\subsection{Geometry as predictive equivalence}
Conditional predictions induce a distance between complete or partial states $x,y$:
\begin{equation}
\theta_\Ph(x,y)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
\sqrt{\JS\!\left(
\phi_{i,c}(\cdot\mid x_{-i}),\phi_{i,c}(\cdot\mid y_{-i})
\right)}.
\label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
Here $\JS$ denotes Jensen--Shannon divergence. The resulting predictive pseudometric distinguishes states by their conditional consequences.

Prior LSM reconstruction reduced mean distance distortion by 54.9\% (GSS) and 60.9\% (Eurobarometer) with 80\% of responses masked \cite{dahal2026}.

For GSS models with registered left and right reference states $s_L,s_R$, DTAG reports
\begin{equation}
I_\Ph(s)=\frac{\theta_\Ph(s_L,s)-\theta_\Ph(s_R,s)}
{\theta_\Ph(s_L,s_R)}.
\label{eq:ideology}
\end{equation}
The reference poles specify opposing responses on 27 GSS 2024 items covering policy, civil liberties, spending, and religion (Table~\ref{tab:poles}) \cite{dahal2026}. Other coordinates are unspecified; $I_\Ph$ measures position in the predictive geometry induced by the LSM.

\section{Adaptive Question Selection}
DTAG separates \emph{question selection} from the \emph{source of the answer}. The LSM conditionals determine the expected geometric effect of each candidate question. In the native simulations, a fixed initial persona anchors the state, the optimizer chooses survey item $v_t$, and the answer is drawn directly from the LSM:
\begin{align}
A_t&\sim p_\Ph(\cdot\mid s_t,v_t),\label{eq:draw}\\
s_{t+1}&=s_t\cup\{v_t=A_t\},\label{eq:update}\\
\calB_{t+1}&=\calB_\Ph(s_{t+1}).\label{eq:posterior}
\end{align}
These native trajectories use no LLM to generate respondents' answers. In the human experiment, the LSM still supplies the directional selection criterion, but the selected question is presented to a participant, whose \emph{observed answer replaces the LSM draw}. A language-model interface renders questions and interprets free-text responses as one or more native survey-variable assignments; these accepted assignments update the conditional state. Thus the displayed single-item update describes the native simulation exactly, while the human implementation can incorporate multiple assignments from one answer. In neither regime does the question-selection algorithm prescribe the answer.

For candidate item $v$ and answer $a$, define
\begin{align}
\Delta_v(a;s)&=I_\Ph(s\cup\{v=a\})-I_\Ph(s),\label{eq:delta}\\
\mu_v(s)&=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_v}p_\Ph(a\mid s,v)\Delta_v(a;s),\label{eq:mu}\\
\sigma_v^2(s)&=\sum_a p_\Ph(a\mid s,v)(\Delta_v(a;s)-\mu_v(s))^2.
\label{eq:var}
\end{align}
Right- and left-seeking policies maximize and minimize $\mu_v(s)$; neutral minimizes $|\mu_v(s)|$, and random samples an eligible item. By construction,
\begin{equation*}
\EE[I_\Ph(s_{t+1})-I_\Ph(s_t)\mid s_t,v_t]=\mu_{v_t}(s_t).
\end{equation*}
Accumulated displacement follows successive selected items and their realized answers, which are LSM draws in simulation and participant responses in the human experiment.

The effect on an unasked item $j$ after observing $(v,a)$ is
\begin{equation}
R_j(s;v,a)=\JS\!\bigl(p_\Ph(\cdot\mid s,j),
p_\Ph(\cdot\mid s\cup\{v=a\},j)\bigr).
\label{eq:ripple}
\end{equation}
A weighted sum $\mathcal P_\Ph(s;v,a)=\sum_{j\notin S\cup\{v\}}w_jR_j(s;v,a)$ measures redistribution across the remaining items. Unlike text retrieval \cite{lewis2020}, DTAG maintains an explicit conditional response state. The public implementation includes a web application (\url{https://github.com/zeroknowledgediscovery/DTAG/tree/main/webapp}).

\section{Refinement and Possible Response Effects}
Under \emph{phenotypic refinement}, successive answers restrict predictions for unasked items. For held-out responses $Y$, the expected information from asking $v$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G(v\mid s)=
H(Y\mid s)-\EE_{A_v\mid s}[H(Y\mid s,v=A_v)].
\label{eq:ig}
\end{equation}
Under a coherent joint model this is conditional mutual information, distinct from directional displacement $\mu_v(s)$. Held-out response prediction evaluates refinement directly.

\subsection{Possible response effects.}
Questioning a person may change later reports. A response--transition kernel separates an observed answer from a possible latent-state update:
\begin{equation}
K_q(a,z'\mid z)=O_q(a\mid z)\,T_q(z'\mid z,a).
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
Passive observation has $T_q(z'\mid z,a)=\delta_z(z')$; shifts in salience or belief can induce nontrivial transitions. This transition model distinguishes the measurement process from possible response-dependent state transitions.

\subsection{Order dependence.}
For fixed realized answers, the terminal index is order-independent; sequentially generated answers need not be. A coherent joint distribution satisfies
\begin{equation}
P(a\mid s,v)P(b\mid s,v=a,w)
=P(b\mid s,w)P(a\mid s,w=b,v).
\label{eq:compat}
\end{equation}
Separately fitted LSM conditionals may violate this identity \cite{heckerman2000}. Fixed-set replays evaluate its consequences for native response sequences.

\section{Native GSS 2024 Computational Experiments}
\label{sec:native}

\subsection{Adaptive questioning from pinned persona states}
We initialized the LSM with six explicitly specified GSS 2024 persona states (Table~\ref{tab:personas}) and generated 40 independent trajectories per persona and question-selection policy (right, left, neutral, random). At each of twenty steps the directional policy selected a survey item from the LSM's predicted effects, then a response was \emph{sampled from that item's LSM conditional distribution} and incorporated into the state. This is an LSM-driven simulation, not an LLM-generated persona dialogue. \emph{All 27 pole-defining items were excluded from question selection} (Table~\ref{tab:poles}). The 960 trajectories therefore comprise 19,200 responses to non-pole items, with pinned starting states and independently sampled answers.




Across all six personas, right- and left-seeking policies moved the index in their intended directions (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch_adaptive}). As pole-defining questions were excluded, the movement results from conditioning on other survey items, not directly asking those used to define the reference index. Neutral and random policies provide comparisons.

\subsection{Question selection as observed paths}
Question identities are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:question-coverage}. The 18 displayed heatmaps contain 161 distinct items across right, left, and neutral selection (Fig.~\ref{fig:question-map}). A shared categorical ordering approximately minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C(\pi)=\sum_{p,u,t}\sum_{r<r'}
\big|\pi(v_{purt})-\pi(v_{pur't})\big|,
\label{eq:question-seriation}
\end{equation}
Here $u$ indexes policies. Heatmap colors identify items, not ideological position; 67 further items occurred only in random selection.



\subsection{Fixed-set replay: robustness to question ordering}
For each persona and right/left/neutral policy, we froze the 20-item sequence from adaptive replicate zero, giving 18 fixed sets. Each set was replayed in original, reversed, and two shuffled orders, drawing 40 fresh response sequences per order directly from the LSM (2,880 runs). Matched question-specific random variates reduced contrast variance.

The direction persisted across replays (Fig.~\ref{fig:order-effects}): right-derived sets yielded mean displacement $+0.0381$ (original) and $+0.0377$ (reversed); left-derived sets yielded $-0.0399$ and $-0.0410$. In both orders, all six persona-specific 95\% bootstrap intervals excluded zero in the prescribed direction. Shuffles followed the same pattern; neutral sets remained near zero.

All 54 pointwise bootstrap intervals for paired mean between-order contrasts included zero; no systematic mean order effect was detected. Selected question sets therefore retained directional displacement without continuous reoptimization.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
DTAG uses high-dimensional survey dependencies to select questions
according to their expected effect on an LSM-defined predictive phenotype.
The native experiments establish this behavior in a fully model-generated
response setting: the initial state is fixed by a specified persona, each
question is selected by the directional policy, and the response is sampled
from the LSM. Fixed-set replays show that the selected questions preserve
the intended displacement across the tested orderings.

The human experiment replaces simulated answers with participants'
responses while retaining LSM-directed selection. The 71 completed
sessions exhibit a marked directional asymmetry: 20/21 Left-push
participants moved left, whereas 21/38 Right-push participants moved
right. Among initially Left-leaning participants assigned Right push,
17/29 showed no net rightward movement at the end of the sequence.
All 17 were identified by their first four answers, with two additional
participants flagged. Together, the simulations and human experiment
show both directional refinement of an inferred worldview and early
identification of a direction-specific resistant response phenotype.

\section{Scope and Limitations}
The index measures an LSM-inferred survey-response phenotype, not an
independent change in underlying political beliefs. LSM conditionals
encode learned predictive dependencies, not causal effects, and
separately estimated conditionals need not define a coherent joint
distribution \cite{heckerman2000}. Accordingly, the human results
describe changes in inferred position as answers are elicited; a
held-out or delayed response battery would be needed to isolate
changes in beliefs from changes in inference. The original four-policy
human cohort was randomized, whereas subsequent fixed-policy cohorts
were recruited separately, so pooled arm comparisons do not constitute
a single randomized causal contrast. The early resistance rule was
evaluated retrospectively in the same 29 initially Left-leaning
Right-push participants and requires validation in an independent cohort.
The fixed-set replay results show no detected systematic mean ordering
effect in the tested sequences rather than establishing general order
invariance.
